# Free Flo Box Product Usage Analysis

## Executive Summary:

Free Flo Box provides free menstrual products, including tampons, pads, menstrual cups, and period underwear, through community pantries in Dayton, Ohio. The organization wanted a dashboard that could clearly show how products are being utilized and help community members and supporters understand the organization's impact.

Using Google Sheets/Excel and Tableau, I analyzed product utilization data from the South Park and St. Anne's Hill pantry locations. I created a Tableau dashboard to compare product usage over time, by location, product type, and reusability.

The analysis shows that product usage varies significantly between the two pantry locations. St. Anne's Hill has a much higher usage of tampons, while South Park has a much higher usage of reusable underwear. These differences provide insight into the types of products being utilized at each location.


### Business Problem:

Free Flo Box tracks the number of menstrual products utilized at its community pantries, but the raw data can be difficult to understand without visualization.

The organization wanted an easy-to-understand dashboard that could help answer questions such as:

- How many products have been provided?
- Which products are used most frequently?
- How does product usage differ between the two pantry locations?
- How does product usage change over time?
- How does disposable product usage compare with reusable product usage?
- Which product types and absorbency levels are used most frequently?

The goal was to turn the raw pantry utilization data into an interactive visualization that could be understood by community members, supporters, and other non-technical audiences.


### Methodology:

1. Reviewed and analyzed the Free Flo Box pantry utilization data from Google Sheets.
2. Organized the data by location, product type, reusability, date, and product size/absorbency.
3. Created Tableau visualizations to analyze monthly product usage and product-level utilization.
4. Built a Tableau dashboard comparing South Park and St. Anne's Hill.
5. Added annotations to highlight important trends and observations within the data.
6. Compared product usage between disposable and reusable products and between the two pantry locations.


### Skills:

**Tableau:** Data visualization, dashboards, calculated fields, aggregation, filtering, annotations, trend analysis, data storytelling

**Excel / Google Sheets:** Data exploration, data organization, working with structured datasets, data analysis

**Data Analysis:** Trend analysis, location comparisons, categorical analysis, interpreting data, communicating insights


### Results & Business Recommendations:

The analysis showed that product usage differs considerably between the two Free Flo Box locations.

At **St. Anne's Hill**, the dashboard shows approximately:

- **23,272 tampons**
- **967 underwear**
- **755 cups**

Tampons represent the largest product category at this location.

At **South Park**, the dashboard shows approximately:

- **15,710 underwear**
- **3,142 tampons**
- **1,571 cups**

Reusable underwear represents the largest product category at this location.

These results show that the two pantry locations have different patterns of product utilization. Looking at each location separately provides more useful information than only looking at the overall product totals.

Based on the analysis, I recommend that Free Flo Box:

1. Continue tracking product usage by pantry location.
2. Monitor product demand by product type and absorbency level.
3. Use monthly usage trends to identify periods of higher product demand.
4. Continue monitoring disposable versus reusable product utilization.
5. Use the dashboard to communicate product demand and community impact to supporters and potential partners.

The dashboard gives Free Flo Box a way to communicate its impact while also providing insight into the types of products that are most needed at each pantry location.


### Next Steps:

6. Continue updating the dashboard as new pantry utilization data becomes available.


### Limitations:

The data represents products utilized and does not represent the number of individual people who used the pantries.

The dataset does not include demographic information, individual user information, or the circumstances of pantry users.

Because the original Google Sheet is continuously updated, product totals may change as new data is added.

Product utilization should also not automatically be interpreted as the number of menstrual cycles served.
